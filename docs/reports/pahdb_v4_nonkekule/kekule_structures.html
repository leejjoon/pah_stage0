Kekulé structures and spin

Whether every carbon in a PAH can get a double bond decides whether the molecule has unpaired electrons. That in turn decides the electronic state a calculation must use. PAHdb v4.00 gets this wrong for 334 molecules.

Every carbon needs one double bond

Each carbon in a PAH has four bonding electrons. Three go into single bonds to its three neighbours. The fourth must pair with the fourth electron of one neighbouring carbon, which makes a double bond.

A Kekulé structure is a way of drawing the molecule so that every carbon has exactly one double bond. When one exists, all electrons are paired and the molecule is an ordinary closed-shell singlet, with total spin 0.

A hexagon network can be coloured with two colours, blue and amber, so that every bond joins one of each. A double bond therefore always pairs a blue carbon with an amber one. If one colour has more carbons, the surplus cannot be paired: those carbons are left over, each with an unpaired electron.

Counting the colours predicts the spin

For these networks, each surplus carbon gives one unpaired electron, and the unpaired electrons line up with the same spin (Lieb's theorem). So the count alone gives the state the calculation should use:

0↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ …singlet (closed-shell)naphthalene; most PAHs
1↑doublet (a radical)phenalenyl; every PAH with an odd number of H

Which carbons end up left over depends on how the double bonds are drawn; press “Draw the bonds another way” above to see them move. The number left over never changes. In the real molecule the unpaired electrons are spread over the majority colour rather than sitting on particular atoms.

What went wrong in PAHdb v4.00

Molecules with two left-over carbons must be computed as triplets. PAHdb v3.20 did that for all 51 it contained, and their spectra are normal. In v4.00, 334 such molecules are listed as singlets, with every electron forced into a pair. 331 of them are new in v4.00. Their computed infrared intensities come out about five times too high, and the CH-stretch band near 3050 cm⁻¹ almost disappears.

In one sentence

A PAH whose carbons cannot all be paired by double bonds has unpaired electrons: one left over makes a radical, two make a triplet. PAHdb v4.00 lists 334 two-left-over molecules as fully paired singlets, and their spectra are wrong as a result.

Data: NASA Ames PAH IR Spectroscopic Database, computational versions 3.20 and 4.00. The colour test, the pairing and the counts are computed in this page from the molecular geometries.
